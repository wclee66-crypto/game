Saerok is a brain-training playground where games that help with early dementia and mild cognitive decline are free to play. It works on a computer, a tablet or a phone, and if the screen is hard to use you can print the puzzles instead.

Five levels

  • 1. First steps — 4 sums · up to 10 · adding and taking away
  • 2. Gentle — 4 sums · up to 20
  • 3. Easy — 5 sums · up to 40
  • 4. Normal — 5 sums · up to 60
  • 5. Hard — 6 sums · up to 99

Scoring

How to playFill in the blanks so that every sum works both across and down. A corner square belongs to two sums, so filling one square helps solve the other sum as well
What goes in a blankAlways a number from 1 to 9, so one tap on the keypad is all it takes
How many blanksOne per sum. Two sums means two blanks, four sums means four
ShapesA fresh shape is worked out every time, so the same one never comes round twice — corners, crosses, square frames, ladders and more
LevelOnly adding and taking away. Each level adds another sum and bigger numbers — Level 1 four sums up to 10 · Level 2 four sums up to 20 · Level 3 five sums up to 40 · Level 4 five sums up to 60 · Level 5 six sums up to 99
Filling inUp to 600 points · in proportion to the squares filled in correctly
Time bonusUp to 300 points · only if you fill them all in, in proportion to the time left
Accuracy bonusUp to 100 points · 10 points less each time you put in a wrong number
Hint penalty50 points off per hint (it fills in one square for you)
If you put the wrong numberIt stays there in red. Erase it and put in another number
Level bonusNormal +100, Hard +250 (when you fill them all in)
Top scoreLevels 1–3: 1,000 · Normal: 1,100 · Hard: 1,250

I went looking online for puzzles for my mother, who lives with dementia, and found it far too hard to find good ones — so I made these for her. I am putting them here in the hope that they help someone else too.
